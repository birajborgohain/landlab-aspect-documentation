August 14, 2026, Badland Day 5 Gasparini & Brandon (2011) and Salles et al. (2018) LEM, Sediment Transport, and the Evolution of Deposition Modeling
====================================================================================================================================================

Papers
------

The main paper is Salles, Ding, and Brocard (2018), ``pyBadlands:
A framework to simulate sediment transport, landscape dynamics and basin
stratigraphic evolution through space and time``.

The paper is available at:

https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0195557

The particularly important cited paper is Reference 18:

Gasparini and Brandon (2011), ``A generalized power law approximation for
fluvial incision of bedrock channels``.

The main study questions are:

* How did Landscape-Evolution Models (LEMs) move from simple empirical
  erosion laws toward more physically meaningful representations of
  sediment transport and deposition?
* Is the improvement simply a matter of changing stream-power exponents,
  or does it involve introducing a different physical representation of
  sediment?

1. What Salles et al. (2018) is trying to do
---------------------------------------------

Salles et al. (2018) develops pyBadlands as a framework for simulating:

* landscape dynamics;
* erosion;
* sediment transport;
* sediment deposition;
* marine sediment redistribution; and
* basin stratigraphic evolution.

The important conceptual shift is that landscape evolution is not treated
only as a problem of erosion or bedrock incision.

Instead, the model attempts to represent the sediment-routing system:

.. code-block:: text

   SOURCE
      |
      v
   erosion
      |
      v
   sediment production
      |
      v
   sediment transport
      |
      v
   deposition
      |
      v
   marine redistribution
      |
      v
   sediment sink / basin
      |
      v
   stratigraphy

This makes the model relevant to stratigraphic evolution because sediment
produced in one location can be transported and deposited somewhere else.

2. The traditional stream-power approach
-----------------------------------------

A classical stream-power incision model can be written as

.. math::

   I = K A^m S^n

where:

* :math:`I` is incision rate;
* :math:`K` is an erodibility or efficiency coefficient;
* :math:`A` is drainage area;
* :math:`S` is channel slope; and
* :math:`m` and :math:`n` are scaling exponents.

This formulation is useful because it is simple and computationally
efficient for large-scale LEM simulations.

However, real rivers also depend strongly on sediment.

Sediment can affect bedrock incision in two opposing ways.

A low to moderate sediment supply can produce a ``tool effect``:

.. code-block:: text

   sediment
       |
       v
   impacts bedrock
       |
       v
   abrasion
       |
       v
   increased incision

Very high sediment supply can produce a ``cover effect``:

.. code-block:: text

   large sediment supply
       |
       v
   bedrock becomes covered
       |
       v
   less bedrock exposure
       |
       v
   reduced incision

Thus sediment is not simply a passive product of erosion. It can also
modify the erosion process itself.

3. What Reference 18 contributes
--------------------------------

Gasparini and Brandon (2011) examines more complicated, sediment-dependent
models of bedrock incision and asks whether their large-scale behavior can
still resemble a simple stream-power law.

A more physically detailed representation can be written schematically as

.. math::

   I = f(A,S,Q_s,\ldots)

where :math:`Q_s` represents sediment flux.

This differs from

.. math::

   I = K A^m S^n.

The sediment-dependent formulation can represent effects such as:

* sediment tools;
* sediment cover;
* sediment transport;
* abrasion; and
* transport limitation.

The important result is that a complicated physical model can nevertheless
produce an effective relationship that looks approximately like

.. math::

   I \approx K' A^{m'} S^{n'}.

Thus a landscape generated by a physically richer sediment-dependent model
can appear, at landscape scale, to follow a simple power-law relationship.

4. Why this is NOT simply changing m and n
------------------------------------------

This is the key point.

Suppose the original model is

.. math::

   I = K A^m S^n.

One approach is to change the exponents:

.. math::

   I = K' A^{0.35} S^{0.8}.

This changes the empirical sensitivity of incision to drainage area and
slope.

However, changing the exponents does not necessarily introduce a new
physical process.

Gasparini and Brandon show something more subtle. A physically richer model
may be written as

.. math::

   I = f(A,S,Q_s).

If sediment flux itself depends on landscape variables,

.. math::

   Q_s = g(A,S,\ldots),

then

.. math::

   I = f[A,S,g(A,S)].

Over a particular range, this complicated relationship may be approximated
by

.. math::

   I \approx K' A^{m'} S^{n'}.

Therefore the effective exponents :math:`m'` and :math:`n'` can emerge from
the underlying physical system.

The important distinction is:

.. code-block:: text

   CHANGING EXPONENTS
   ------------------

   I = K A^m S^n

   changes the effective scaling relationship.

   It does NOT necessarily introduce
   a new physical process.


   ADDING SEDIMENT FLUX
   --------------------

   I = f(A, S, Qs)

   introduces sediment as an explicit
   control on incision.

5. Interpretation of the effective exponents
---------------------------------------------

The effective exponents can be interpreted as measures of the sensitivity
of incision to drainage area and slope.

Schematically,

.. math::

   m' \sim \frac{\partial \ln I}{\partial \ln A}

and

.. math::

   n' \sim \frac{\partial \ln I}{\partial \ln S}.

Thus the exponents can describe the local response of a more complicated
incision system.

They should not automatically be interpreted as fundamental constants that
uniquely identify a physical erosion mechanism.

Gasparini and Brandon found that effective exponents can depend on the
conditions under which the landscape evolves, including the spatial pattern
of rock uplift.

This gives an important warning:

   Similar stream-power exponents do not necessarily imply identical
   underlying erosion physics.

6. The transition toward explicit sediment modeling
----------------------------------------------------

The conceptual development can be summarized as:

.. code-block:: text

   LEVEL 1
   -------
   Simple stream-power model

       I = K A^m S^n

           |
           v

   LEVEL 2
   -------
   Recognition that sediment matters

       I = f(A, S, Qs)

           |
           v

   LEVEL 3
   -------
   More physical sediment-dependent incision

       tool effect
       +
       cover effect
       +
       transport limitation

           |
           v

   LEVEL 4
   -------
   Explicit sediment routing

       erosion
          |
          v
       sediment flux
          |
          v
       transport capacity
          |
          v
       erosion / transport / deposition

           |
           v

   LEVEL 5
   -------
   Source-to-sink and stratigraphic evolution

       landscape
          |
          v
       river system
          |
          v
       coastal / marine system
          |
          v
       basin
          |
          v
       stratigraphy

The important development is therefore not simply changing the exponents.
It is progressively introducing sediment as an explicit component of the
landscape-evolution system.

7. Sediment supply versus transport capacity
---------------------------------------------

For the deposition problem, one of the most important ideas in the Salles
et al. framework is the distinction between sediment supply and sediment
transport capacity.

Let

.. math::

   Q_s = \text{actual sediment flux}

and

.. math::

   Q_t = \text{sediment transport capacity}.

The model can distinguish several regimes.

Case 1: :math:`Q_s < Q_t`
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The sediment supply is smaller than the amount of sediment that the flow
can transport.

.. code-block:: text

   sediment supply < transport capacity
                    |
                    v
             available capacity
                    |
                    v
             sediment can move
               downstream

Case 2: :math:`Q_s = Q_t`
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The sediment supply approximately matches transport capacity. The system is
at transport capacity.

Case 3: :math:`Q_s > Q_t`
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The sediment supply exceeds transport capacity.

.. code-block:: text

   sediment supply > transport capacity
                    |
                    v
             excess sediment
                    |
                    v
                deposition

This provides a more physically interpretable basis for deposition than
simply selecting an exponent that produces more deposition.

8. Why this matters for deposition modeling
-------------------------------------------

The conceptual transition is:

.. code-block:: text

   OLD APPROACH

   prescribe surface response
           |
           v
   use empirical erosion/deposition
   relationship


   MORE EXPLICIT APPROACH

   sediment production
           |
           v
   sediment flux Qs
           |
           v
   transport capacity Qt
           |
           v
   compare Qs and Qt
           |
       +---+---+
       |       |
     Qs<Qt   Qs>Qt
       |       |
   transport deposition
       |       |
       +---+---+
           |
           v
     downstream sediment
           |
           v
       basin filling

The latter approach allows deposition to emerge from the balance between
sediment supply and transport capacity.

9. Tool effect and cover effect
-------------------------------

A physically richer sediment-dependent incision model does not assume that
more sediment always means more erosion.

Tool effect
~~~~~~~~~~~

Sediment acts as tools that impact and abrade bedrock:

.. code-block:: text

   sediment tools
        |
        v
   bedrock impacts
        |
        v
      abrasion
        |
        v
   greater incision

Cover effect
~~~~~~~~~~~~

Excess sediment covers the bed:

.. code-block:: text

   excess sediment
        |
        v
   bedrock cover
        |
        v
   reduced bed exposure
        |
        v
   reduced incision

Thus sediment can both enhance and inhibit incision depending on the
sediment regime.

10. What Salles et al. adds beyond the incision problem
--------------------------------------------------------

Gasparini and Brandon primarily help explain the relationship between
sediment-dependent bedrock incision and the apparent stream-power law.

Salles et al. take the problem further by developing a framework for
landscape and sedimentary-basin evolution.

The framework includes processes associated with:

* detachment-limited incision;
* transport-limited incision;
* sediment-flux-dependent incision;
* tool effects;
* cover effects;
* sediment transport;
* deposition;
* marine sediment redistribution; and
* stratigraphic accumulation.

The major conceptual shift is:

.. code-block:: text

   FROM

   erosion law

   TO

   sediment-routing system

The latter is much more appropriate when the objective is basin development
and stratigraphy.

              coast
                |

             sediment

The location where sediment is produced is not necessarily the location

12. Relevance to ASPECT--Landlab coupling
------------------------------------------

This conceptual development is highly relevant to an ASPECT--Landlab
sediment model.

Instead of beginning with:

   What values should be used for :math:`m` and :math:`n`?

a more physically meaningful framework starts with:

#. What is the sediment supply?

   .. math::

      Q_s

#. What is the transport capacity?

   .. math::

      Q_t

#. What happens when sediment supply exceeds transport capacity?

   .. math::

      Q_s > Q_t

#. How is the excess sediment stored?

   Conceptually,

   .. math::

      Q_{\mathrm{dep}} \sim Q_s - Q_t

   subject to the specific transport formulation and conservation equations
   used by the model.

At a simple conceptual level,

.. math::

   \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial t}
   =
   \text{uplift}
   -
   \text{erosion}
   +
   \text{deposition}.

The deposited sediment can then become part of an evolving stratigraphic
record.

13. Key distinction for future LEM studies
-------------------------------------------

The following distinction should be retained when reading LEM literature.

.. list-table:: Parameterization versus physical-process improvement
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 70

   * - Approach
     - Meaning
   * - Change :math:`m` and :math:`n`
     - Changes the effective scaling of erosion with drainage area and slope.
   * - Add sediment flux :math:`Q_s`
     - Introduces sediment as an explicit control on incision.
   * - Add tool effect
     - Allows sediment to enhance bedrock abrasion.
   * - Add cover effect
     - Allows excessive sediment to suppress bedrock incision.
   * - Add transport capacity :math:`Q_t`
     - Provides a physical basis for determining whether sediment can be
       transported.
   * - Compare :math:`Q_s` and :math:`Q_t`
     - Allows deposition to emerge when sediment supply exceeds transport
       capacity.
   * - Conserve sediment mass
     - Connects erosion, transport, deposition, and downstream sediment
       routing.
   * - Add marine routing and storage
     - Extends the LEM into a source-to-sink and stratigraphic framework.

14. Main lesson from Reference 18
---------------------------------

The central lesson from Gasparini and Brandon (2011) is:

   A simple stream-power relationship can emerge as an approximation to a
   much more complicated sediment-dependent incision process.

Therefore:

   Effective stream-power exponents should not automatically be treated as
   direct fingerprints of the underlying physical erosion mechanism.

This prevents an overly simple interpretation:

.. code-block:: text

   different exponent
          =
   different physical process

That equivalence is not generally valid.

15. Main lesson from Salles et al. (2018)
-----------------------------------------

The central contribution relevant to stratigraphic modeling is broader:

   Landscape evolution can be represented as a connected sediment-routing
   problem rather than only as a topographic-incision problem.

The important chain is:

.. code-block:: text

   uplift
      |
      v
   erosion
      |
      v
   sediment production
      |
      v
   sediment flux
      |
      v
   transport capacity
      |
      v
   erosion / transport / deposition
      |
      v
   sediment redistribution
      |
      v
   sediment storage
      |
      v
   stratigraphy

16. Research questions to carry forward
----------------------------------------

For future Strat and LEM paper studies, ask:

#. What physical process is being represented?
#. Is the process merely parameterized differently?
#. Are the stream-power exponents being changed?
#. Is sediment flux explicitly represented?
#. Is transport capacity explicitly represented?
#. Does deposition emerge from a sediment-mass balance?
#. Is sediment conserved as it moves downstream?
#. Can sediment be remobilized?
#. Does the model connect erosion to deposition?
#. Does it connect the terrestrial system to the basin?
#. Does the final model produce an evolving stratigraphic record?

This framework helps trace how LEMs have progressively developed from simple
empirical erosion equations toward coupled sediment-routing and
stratigraphic models.

Key takeaway
------------

The major advancement is not simply changing power-law exponents.

The deeper development is making sediment an explicit part of the physical
system, allowing sediment supply, transport capacity, erosion, deposition,
redistribution, and storage to interact.

.. note::

   Now the question is no longer simply *"what deposition
   equation does Badlands/Fastscape/Landlab use?"* but:

   **How did LEMs move from prescribing surface change with simple empirical
   power laws toward explicitly conserving and routing sediment, with
   deposition emerging from the competition between sediment supply and
   transport capacity?**

   That is the thread I would follow through
   **Gasparini & Brandon → Salles/Badlands → Davy & Lague → Hobley/Yuan →
   Fastscape/Landlab**, because it will let us identify *exactly* what each
   generation of LEM added rather than treating all "deposition models" as
   equivalent.

   Need a close look to explicitly distinguish **"changed
   parameterization/exponents"** from **"introduced a new physical
   process."**
